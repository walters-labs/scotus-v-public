# Affirmative action: admissions-specific polling

Updated November 12, 2026. Rebuild with `make affirmative-action-policy`.

## Fisher II (June 23, 2016)

The former CES item combined employment and admissions. It remains in the research
input but is no longer selected for Fisher II. The pane now keeps these two
higher-education questions separate:

| Source | Fieldwork | Base | Published categories |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Gallup Q24 | June 13–July 5, 2013 | 4,373 adults | Merit alone 67%; consider race/ethnicity 28%; no opinion 5% |
| Pew C127 | February 27–March 16, 2014 | 3,335 adults | Good thing 63%; bad thing 30%; don't know/refused 8% |

[Gallup original questionnaire, page 3](https://news.gallup.com/file/poll/163679/Affirmative_Action_130724.pdf)
offers rotated alternatives: merit alone despite low minority enrollment, versus
racial/ethnic consideration for diversity even if some minority applicants would
otherwise not be admitted. The apparent merit/diversity opposition is the survey's
framing, not a factual premise adopted by the app. Results are nationally weighted;
the telephone study recontacted Gallup Daily respondents and oversampled Black and
Hispanic adults. Use national totals, not subgroup rows.

[Pew original topline, page 3](https://assets.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/legacy-questionnaires/4-22-14%20Affirmative%20Action%20Topline.pdf)
asks all respondents about programs aimed at increasing minority representation
on campuses. It does not specify race as an admissions criterion. The printed
63/30/8 total is 101 due to rounding: retain it, do not infer 7% uncertainty.
Methodology and total sample are on pages 1–2. The older 2003 row is not imported.

Both questions are contextual to [Fisher II](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/14-981).
Neither describes the complete Texas system, which combined a percentage-admission
pathway and holistic review including race. The favorable answers do not measure
approval of the constitutional reasoning or the specific challenged program.
The two sources differ in year, wording and sampling design; their difference
cannot be attributed solely to wording, treated as a trend, or pooled.

## Timing exclusions

The 2013 Gallup fieldwork ended after Fisher I on June 24, 2013. Fisher I remains
procedural/not comparable; even a contextual policy selector would reject this
survey as earlier evidence. The
[2016 Gallup survey](https://news.gallup.com/poll/193508/oppose-colleges-considering-race-admissions.aspx)
ran June 7–July 1, straddling Fisher II. It is not imported as pre-ruling opinion.

to the Harvard/UNC decision, with its complete six-category distribution. Earlier
2013/2014 observations are outside that decision's three-year window. The VOTER
2020 item remains explicitly broad context rather than a direct admissions match.
