## Gregg v. Georgia (1976-07-02)

[Decision source](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/428/153/)

General support for capital punishment does not establish support for every death sentence or method of execution.

### Permissibility of capital punishment for murder

**maintains_permission · direct**

Rejected the claim that death is invariably unconstitutional as punishment for murder.

Permits capital punishment under constitutional constraints; does not require a death sentence for every murder.

GSS asks about the same broad punishment/offense pairing. This supports only a broad policy-direction comparison, not approval of the constitutional reasoning.

Source locator: Opinion of Stewart, Powell and Stevens, Part III, 428 U.S. 168–187.

## Atkins v. Virginia (2002-06-20)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/536/304)

The survey does not define intellectual disability or measure respondents’ views on diagnostic criteria. It was completed before the ruling; it does not establish that opinion caused the decision.

### Exemption for intellectual disability

**expands · direct**

Prohibited executing offenders with intellectual disability.

A categorical limit on who may be executed, not abolition of capital punishment.

The question addresses execution of the same broad offender category. Opposition aligns with the exemption’s policy direction, not necessarily the constitutional reasoning or clinical eligibility rules.
