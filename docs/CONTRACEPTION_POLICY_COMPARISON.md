## Burwell v. Hobby Lobby Stores (2014-06-30)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/13-354)

The 5–4 judgment concerned statutory religious protections. The direction label describes the coverage mandate only, not a numerical balance between religious liberty and employee access.

### Religious objections to employer coverage

**restricts · contextual**

The Court held that applying the contraceptive mandate to the objecting closely held companies violated RFRA.

This limited enforcement of the employer mandate; it did not prohibit contraceptive use or invalidate the entire coverage requirement.

Both polls address religious objections and employee coverage. They do not isolate closely held corporations, the four disputed methods, or alternative ways to provide coverage.

Source locator: Alito majority opinion, Parts III–V, especially V.B.

## Zubik v. Burwell (2016-05-16)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/14-1418)

### Accommodation for religious nonprofits

**no_merits_ruling · not_comparable**

The Court vacated the judgments and remanded to allow consideration of an accommodation.

The Court expressly did not decide the merits of the RFRA challenge.

General preferences about coverage cannot turn this remand into approval or rejection of an exemption.

Source locator: Per curiam opinion, paragraphs beginning “In light of the positions” and “The Court expresses no view”.
