## District of Columbia v. Heller (2008-06-26)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/07-290.ZS.html)

The judgment was 5–4. The individual-right premise and home-possession application are related parts of one ruling, not independent outcomes to count. Storage remains unmeasured.

### Keeping a handgun at home

**expands · contextual**

The Court invalidated D.C.’s handgun ban and recognized an individual right to keep a firearm for self-defense at home.

The right is not unlimited. This did not invalidate all firearm regulation.

Gallup asks about possession generally; Pew asks about sales. Neither isolates D.C.’s home-possession rule or asks whether the Constitution protects it.

### An individual right, independent of militia service

**expands · direct**

The Court interpreted the Second Amendment as protecting an individual right independent of service in a militia.

This is the constitutional premise of the home-possession ruling, not an additional independent outcome or an unlimited right.

Gallup directly asks the individual-versus-militia distinction. It does not specify handguns, home self-defense, storage or state and local application; direct fit is limited to this distinction.

Source locator: Syllabus holding 1; majority opinion Part II.

## McDonald v. City of Chicago (2010-06-28)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/08-1521.ZS.html)

Five justices agreed on the result. Four relied on due process; Thomas used the Privileges or Immunities Clause. General policy preferences do not adjudicate that distinction.

### Applying the right to state and local governments

**expands · contextual**

The Court held that the right recognized in Heller applies against state and local governments, reversing and remanding.

The case extended the constitutional protection; it did not resolve every local firearm rule.

Earlier opposition to handgun bans provides policy context, not an answer to the constitutional incorporation question. Pew’s 2008 observation is reused background, not a new 2010 measurement.

Source locator: Alito opinion, Parts III–IV; Thomas concurrence.
