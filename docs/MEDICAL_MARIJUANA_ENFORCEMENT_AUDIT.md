# Medical marijuana: federal-enforcement polling audit

November 12, 2026. A bounded search found no verified national federal-enforcement
question eligible for the current pre-Raich comparison. This is a finding about
this search, not proof that no suitable poll exists.

Raich was decided June 6, 2005. The existing rule accepts survey years 2002–2005,
with verified dated fieldwork ending before the judgment. Do not widen that rule
for one topic, import later responses as earlier opinion, or substitute support
for medical availability for a preference about federal enforcement.

## Leads reviewed

| Candidate | Evidence and disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Zogby / NORML Foundation, 2001 | Sponsor's December 6, 2001 release describes opposition to federal closure of state-authorized dispensaries. Better policy fit, but outside the 2002–2005 window, and after Oakland's May 2001 ruling. Not imported. |
| Mason-Dixon / Marijuana Policy Project, June 2005 | A reproduced Angus Reid report gives June 8–11 fieldwork and explicitly asks about prosecution after the Supreme Court decision. Post-Raich, and original questionnaire not verified here. Not imported. |
| HCD Research / Muhlenberg, 2005 | References cite a June 9 release about medical use and federal prosecution. Release date alone does not establish fieldwork dates; original questionnaire, sampling and full responses not verified. Unresolved lead, not imported. |

| Time/CNN 2002; AARP 2004 | Search leads concerned medical use generally; the AARP population also differs. They do not establish the missing federal-enforcement preference. No new observation imported. |

An additional secondary search result dates a similar Zogby result to December
2003, while the sponsor's matching report is dated 2001. Do not treat the secondary
date as a verified new survey or manufacture a 2003 observation.

## Result for the app

Keep the verified Pew 2001 and Gallup 2003 medical-use snapshots, labeled contextual.
The overview and Raich explanation now state the missing enforcement evidence
explicitly. No percentages, observations, date windows or match classifications
were changed. The rebuilt artifact records the wording change and its input hash.

[Raich's opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/03-1454)
addresses congressional authority over the local activity. Even a future question
about whether officials *should* prosecute would measure a policy preference,
not necessarily respondents' view of what Congress constitutionally *may* regulate.

Stop here for this pass. Reopen the search if an original, dated pre-ruling
questionnaire becomes available. A separately labeled after-ruling section could
be a future editorial choice, but is not silently added to pre-ruling comparisons.
