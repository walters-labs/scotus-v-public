## United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers’ Cooperative (2001-05-14)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/00-151.ZS.html)

The judgment was unanimous among eight participating justices; three concurred separately. The holding addressed manufacture and distribution, not every possible patient-possession defense.

### Medical necessity as a defense for distribution

**maintains_restriction · contextual**

The Court rejected a medical-necessity exception to the federal prohibitions on manufacturing and distributing marijuana.

A statutory question about the distributor’s defense, not a scientific judgment about medical benefit.

The poll asks about physician-directed medical use, not whether courts can recognize this defense under the statute.

Source locator: Thomas opinion, Part II; Stevens concurrence.

## Gonzales v. Raich (2005-06-06)

[Decision source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/03-1454)

The judgment was 6–3, with a separate concurrence. The 2001 Pew poll falls outside the three-year comparison window and is not reused here.

### Federal authority over state-authorized medical use

**maintains_restriction · contextual**

The Court held that Congress could prohibit local cultivation and use even when medically recommended and permitted by state law.

Federal power to enforce the Controlled Substances Act, not an instruction that Congress must prohibit medical use.

Support for medical use provides context. It does not answer the Commerce Clause question or directly measure approval of federal enforcement.

Source locator: Stevens majority opinion, Parts III–IV; Scalia concurrence.
