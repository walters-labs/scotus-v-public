# Ten policy comparisons: quality checklist

Updated November 12, 2026. This is a roadmap, not an autonomous goal queue.

See [the panel-by-panel quality review](POLICY_QUALITY_REVIEW.md) for evidence
counts, publication limits and the ordered next steps.

Marriage equality is the reference for clarity and traceability. Equal quality
does not require identical amounts of historical polling, but the newer panels
should not be described as equally deep yet.

| Panel | Current evidence | Main remaining improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marriage equality | Multi-source history and component-specific polls | Publication review |
| Abortion | Multi-source history and targeted questions | Publication review |
| Capital punishment | GSS general policy plus targeted Gallup exemption questions | Seek independent corroboration and exact age-at-offense wording |
| Affirmative action | Gallup 2013 / Pew 2014 higher-education questions and Pew 2023 admissions | Seek questions specifying the actual selection mechanism; preserve wording differences |
| Voter ID | One verified pre-Crawford poll | Add independently sourced context/history if available |
| Handgun rights | Three distinct observations, two decisions | Seek specific storage evidence; do not conflate sales and possession |
| Contraception | Two pre-Hobby Lobby sources plus legal timeline | Further coverage history; earlier access polls remain missing |
| Medical marijuana | Earlier medical-use polls for two distinct rulings | Seek more specific federal-enforcement preferences |
| School religion | 1999 graduation and 2001 after-hours questions | Improve graduation/football and student/adult-led group fit |
| Campaign finance | Two earlier broad regulatory polls; separate disclosure component | Weakest policy fit: seek independent-spending and aggregate-donor questions |

Ten local panels are now available. Review them together before adding more.
Campaign finance is provisional context, not evidence of public agreement with
Citizens United or McCutcheon. A panel can be removed or narrowed if its question
fit does not justify publication.

For every addition:

- State a concrete public-policy question and distinguish legal components.
- Use primary opinions and accurately describe procedural outcomes.
- Preserve dates, population, response categories, source organization and
  original denominators, including split samples and item nonresponse.
- Obtain specific earlier questions; do not substitute generic attitudes.
- Explain disagreements and missing evidence without averaging them away.
- Use historical trends only where comparable observations support them.
- Verify reproducibility, packaging and existing panels before deployment.

Next review should strengthen the thinner panels before further additions.
Ten is the desired scope, not a reason to lower the evidence standard.
