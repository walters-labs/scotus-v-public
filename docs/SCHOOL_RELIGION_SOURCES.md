## Santa Fe: replace the graduation proxy

[Freedom Forum, State of the First Amendment 2000](https://www.freedomforum.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/sofa_2000_report.pdf),
PDF page 28 (printed 22), Q33: student-led prayer over a public-address system at
school-sponsored events including football games. Fieldwork April 13–26, 2000,
1,015 national adults, University of Connecticut telephone survey. Methodology:
printed pages 35–37. Full published shares: 44 strongly agree, 20 mildly agree,
15 mildly disagree, 20 strongly disagree, 2 unsure/refused. Total 101 is retained
rounding, not normalized. Fieldwork predates June 19 despite later publication.

This replaces the selected 1999 graduation question. It is closer in setting,
sponsorship and delivery method, but does not spell out the election process
or full school policy described in the [opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-62.ZO.html).
Keep contextual fit. The original graduation record remains in research inputs,
including its bounded less-than-0.5% category, but is not displayed.

## Good News Club: distinguish student groups from religious groups generally

[Pew’s April 2001 report](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/legacy-pdf/15.pdf),
PDF page 75 (printed 74), Q44 Form 1 and Q45 Form 2. Fieldwork March 5–18, 2001,
before the June 11 ruling. The national telephone sample of 2,041 includes a
197-person African-American oversample and uses weights; methods begin on printed
page 52. Use the item bases, not the full survey base:

- Student religious organizations: n=1,023; 17 object, 81 do not object, 2 unsure/refused.
- Religious organizations generally: n=1,018; 22 object, 75 do not object, 3 unsure/refused.

These are two forms within one study, not independent corroboration or a time
trend. No significance claim or causal wording-effect estimate is made. Gallup’s
February 2001 student-group poll remains a separate source. Neither wording
specifies adult leaders, elementary-age children, parental permission or all the
forum conditions described in the [syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-2036.ZS.html).
Not objecting to access is not equivalent to requiring access under the Constitution.

Both PDF question pages were visually checked against the extracted text.
Regression checks cover dates, item bases, retained categories/rounding, excluded
graduation proxy, unchanged contextual labels and reproducible output. No post-ruling
reaction poll or SCOTUSPoll observation is included.
