Abortion · A closer look

Legal under what circumstances—and protected by whom?

Abortion opinion is not one yes-or-no question. Support for legal access, public funding, specific restrictions and keeping Roe can point in different directions.

What the surveys tell us

These sources ask about different policies. We keep their response choices intact: “legal in most cases” is not the same as “for any reason,” and neither directly answers whether a waiting period should be allowed.

What the rulings tell us

Roe established constitutional protection; Casey retained its core while allowing some restrictions; Dobbs removed that federal protection. Funding and provider requirements raise separate questions.

Where the evidence is still thin

The new 1992 Times Mirror questions address notification and waiting requirements. Parental consent remains contextual because the question omits judicial bypass. Funding restrictions in Harris, provider requirements in Hellerstedt, and Mississippi’s specific fifteen-week limit still lack matched questions. Broad legality preferences do not fill those gaps. Missing data below means no suitable question mapped here, not that no such poll exists.
