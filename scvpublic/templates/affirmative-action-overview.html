Affirmative action · A closer look

The goal and the admissions rule are different questions

People can evaluate efforts to increase minority representation differently from a particular way of choosing applicants. These surveys ask about different policies; they do not yield one interchangeable approval score.

Before Fisher’s 2016 ruling

Pew asked whether programs to increase minority representation on campuses were good or bad. Gallup offered a choice between admission “solely on merit” and considering race and ethnicity for diversity. Neither described Texas’s particular admissions system.

Before the 2023 ruling

Pew asked specifically about race and ethnicity in selective-college admissions for diversity. That is closer to the policy at issue than general affirmative-action attitudes, but still does not measure approval of the Harvard and UNC decisions.

What the comparison can tell us

Fisher upheld a particular use of race within holistic review alongside Texas’s percentage-admission pathway. Broad approval of campus representation and a forced choice about admission criteria do not resolve whether respondents supported that system.

The 2013 Gallup survey finished after Fisher’s first ruling, but before the 2016 ruling. A June–July 2016 Gallup survey straddled the second ruling and is excluded. Fisher’s 2013 remand remains unscored.
