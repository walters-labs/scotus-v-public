Campaign finance · A closer look

Which money, which limit?

A donor’s total contributions and a corporation’s independent advertising are different questions. We have specific earlier polling about the candidate limits at issue in McCutcheon; the evidence for Citizens United remains broader context.

Independent spending · 2010

Citizens United allowed independent corporate and union election spending. It did not remove the ban on direct corporate donations, and it upheld the challenged disclosure and disclaimer requirements.

Total donations · 2014

McCutcheon removed aggregate limits across candidates and committees. Limits on what a donor could give each recipient remained. This is the distinction the newer polling helps illuminate.

A closer match, with limits

The first question names a total cap across candidates, rather than a candidate’s fundraising budget. The second asks about free-speech rights. Together they describe views about the disputed kind of limit; neither gives an approval rating for the later decision.

Why this remains a contextual comparison

The poll quoted a $46,200 candidate cap. The ruling discussed an updated $48,600 candidate cap and a $123,200 combined cap covering candidates and other committees. The poll did not cover that full structure or expressly state the cap’s time period. The speech question followed questions about contribution amounts and corruption.

“Too low” is not the same answer as “no limit.” The categories are not collapsed into support for the Court. This is one matched online sample, not a trend or independent corroboration.

What is still missing for Citizens United?

The earlier Gallup question contrasts contribution limits with freedom to support campaigns. It does not isolate independent spending. No reviewed earlier question measures the disclosure rules. These gaps remain visible; later reactions to the decision are not substituted for earlier public opinion.

Gallup’s 2009 chart reports two categories totaling 93%. Its remaining 7% is displayed as an unidentified remainder, not relabeled as uncertainty.
