Capital punishment · A closer look

Supporting the death penalty does not mean supporting every execution

The Court allowed capital punishment under safeguards in Gregg, then excluded particular groups in Atkins and Roper. Earlier surveys let us compare these different policy questions separately.

The general policy

GSS’s 1975 question asks about execution for murder convictions. It broadly matches Gregg’s permissibility question, but does not measure support for Georgia’s sentencing procedures.

The exemptions

Gallup asked separately about intellectual disability and juveniles in May 2002. Majorities opposed execution for both groups. That policy direction is consistent with the later exemptions, without establishing agreement with the Court’s reasoning.

How closely do the questions match?

Atkins: the survey names the relevant disability category, using historical terminology, but does not define clinical eligibility. Roper: “juveniles” is not explicitly defined as under 18 at the time of the offense. That comparison remains contextual, and the poll predates Roper by almost three years.

The 1975 GSS and 2002 Gallup results come from different surveys and periods. They cannot establish a change over time or a causal effect on the Court.
