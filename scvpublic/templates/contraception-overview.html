Contraception · A closer look

Legal access and insurance coverage are different questions

The right to use contraception does not settle who must cover its cost. These selected rulings move from access to disputes over employer coverage and religious objections.

Access: 1965 and 1972

Griswold protected married couples’ contraceptive use. Eisenstadt addressed unequal access for unmarried people. We have not verified suitable earlier polls for those decisions.

Coverage: 2014 and 2016

Hobby Lobby limited the employer mandate as applied to objecting closely held companies. Zubik sent a nonprofit dispute back without deciding the merits.

Before Hobby Lobby, the surveys differed

KFF · May 2014

53% favored requiring coverage despite religious objections; 41% opposed, 2% chose neither/other, and 4% were unsure or refused.

YouGov / Huffington Post · June 2014

40% favored requiring coverage; 44% opposed and 16% were unsure. Neither position had majority support.

KFF explicitly described the trade-off between owners’ beliefs and employees paying themselves. YouGov asked whether objecting private businesses should provide coverage. Timing, wording and survey design differ; we have not isolated why the results differ. Their percentages are not averaged.

These are two earlier snapshots, not a trend or a poll of approval of the Court. Full categories, sample bases and source documents appear below.
