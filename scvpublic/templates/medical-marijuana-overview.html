Medical marijuana · A closer look

Medical use—and who has the authority to permit it?

Earlier polls showed broad support for medically directed marijuana use. The Court’s decisions addressed different questions: a defense under federal law, and Congress’s power to enforce that law.

The public-policy question

Could doctors be allowed to prescribe marijuana for patients? Pew and Gallup asked about medical use specifically. These are not general-legalization polls.

The legal questions

Oakland Cannabis concerned medical necessity as a defense for manufacture and distribution. Raich concerned federal authority over local activity permitted by state law.

Strong medical-use support does not settle the legal issue

It is possible to favor a change in drug policy while accepting Congress’s authority over it. These comparisons show the tension between the policy preference and the restriction left in place; they do not establish that respondents rejected the Court’s reasoning.

Two historical snapshots, from different sources. No pooled score or continuous trend is inferred. “Prescribe” describes the survey wording, not a statement about what federal law allowed.
