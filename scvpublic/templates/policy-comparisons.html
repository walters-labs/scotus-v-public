How to read this comparison

Direct comparison: a question addresses a named policy component. Context only: related preferences, not approval of the specific holding. Missing data: no suitable earlier observation in this selection. Procedural rulings can be not comparable.

We use the latest eligible survey year per source within three years, keeping tied question variants. Same-year surveys are included only when verified fieldwork ends before the ruling. Percentages retain each survey’s wording and denominator; they are not pooled. Court interpretations are explicit source-based assessments, not automated classifications. Votes are not treated as policy magnitude.

These are historical outcomes. No causal, predictive, or statistical-significance claim is made. Missing matches do not mean no relevant survey exists elsewhere.

Marriage equality · A closer look

Should same-sex couples be allowed to marry?

Public support and legal protection changed over time. But permission to marry, recognition of a marriage, and equal treatment within marriage are different questions.

What the surveys tell us

Support rises between the selected 2012 and 2016 observations in CES, GSS and PRRI. The levels differ across sources, whose wording and response categories are not identical.

What the rulings tell us

General marriage preferences match Obergefell’s licensing ruling. Targeted Gallup and ABC/Post questions also address federal and interstate recognition. Birth-certificate rules still have only broad marriage-attitude context.

Why is VOTER lower in 2016?

VOTER records 49.9% favor, 35.1% oppose, 14.6% unsure and 0.3% skipped, using its supplied cross-sectional weights. Among favor/oppose respondents only, support is 58.7%; CES reports 61.6% on that narrower denominator. This explains most of their apparent difference, but does not make the surveys equivalent.

We verified VOTER’s raw response counts and weights against its survey guide. It interviewed returning panelists in November–December 2016. Differences from GSS and PRRI remain; timing, question format and sample design may matter, but we have not isolated their effects. The bars retain the original denominators.
