School religion · A closer look

Who is speaking—the school or a private group?

The Court restricted a school’s football-game prayer policy, then protected a private religious club’s after-hours access. Earlier polls address these two settings separately; a single pro-religion or anti-religion score would hide the distinction.

School-sponsored events · 2000

Freedom Forum asked about students leading prayer over a public-address system at school-sponsored events, including football games. That is closer to Santa Fe than the earlier graduation question, but omits its election procedure and full policy.

After-hours access · 2001

Pew asked separate groups about access for student religious organizations and religious organizations generally. The latter avoids assuming a student-led club, but neither describes Good News Club’s adult leaders, young children or parental-permission rules.

What these answers do—and do not—tell us

The event-prayer question measures permission for a named practice, not approval of Santa Fe’s full policy. Not objecting to a religious group’s access does not establish that respondents thought access was constitutionally required. Neither question is a poll of approval of the later decision.

These are selected historical rulings and earlier preferences, not a guide to every later change in school-religion law. Full question notes, source links and response distributions follow.
